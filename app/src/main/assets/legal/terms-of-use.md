## Not a safety device

**RemoteEar is not a baby monitor, a medical device, a security system, or a safety device, and must
not be relied on as one.** It is a convenience: it plays one room's microphone to your headphones.

It can stop working at any moment, and it can stop *silently*, for reasons outside its control:

- your headphones leave Bluetooth range, or their battery runs out
- the phone's battery runs out, or the phone restarts or overheats
- the phone's manufacturer shuts the app down to save battery — many Android phones do this
  aggressively, and there is no way for an app to prevent it
- a phone call arrives, or another app takes the microphone
- Bluetooth interference, an operating-system update, or a change to app permissions

The app tries hard to tell you when it has stopped: that is what the ongoing notification and the
paused states exist for, and why every one of them says whether you are hearing the room. But **it
cannot guarantee that you will notice the message, or that the phone is still running at all.**

**Never use RemoteEar as the only means of supervising a child, an elderly person, or anyone who
depends on being heard.** Stay within reach, and check in person.

## Hearing

Audio plays at your phone's media volume. Listening at high volume through earbuds for long periods
can damage your hearing. Set the volume with the phone's buttons before you leave the room.

## Listening to a room where other people are

You are responsible for how you use the app. Listening to a room that other people are in may
require their agreement, and the law differs from country to country. RemoteEar records nothing —
but it does let you hear a room you are not in.
