**Phase 2 go/no-go gate.** First time RemoteEar audio ran on hardware.

| | |
|---|---|

## Routing check

The whole point of this scenario: audio being audible does not prove it came from the *right*
microphone.

| | Observed | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| `record.routedDevice.type` | `BUILTIN_MIC` (15) | **pass** |
| `track.routedDevice.type` | `BLUETOOTH_A2DP` (8) | **pass** |
| Any `TYPE_BLUETOOTH_SCO` seen? | **No** | **pass** |
| Negotiated rate / channels | 48 000 Hz mono PCM 16-bit | pass — no resampling |
| Record / track buffer bytes | `minRecord=3840 → 7680`, `minTrack=20622 → 41244` | **see finding below** |

Raw evidence:

```text
device=OnePlus CPH2399 api=34
UNPROCESSED supported=false using=MIC
output sample rate property=48000
output frames per buffer=256
chosen input  = BUILTIN_MIC id=16
chosen output = BLUETOOTH_A2DP id=10479
buffers: minRecord=3840 -> 7680, minTrack=20622 -> 41244
  out device: BUILTIN_SPEAKER(2) id=2
  out device: BUILTIN_EARPIECE(1) id=3
  out device: TELEPHONY(18) id=13
  out device: BLUETOOTH_A2DP(8) id=10479
  out device: BLUETOOTH_SCO(7) id=10469
routing asserted: in=BUILTIN_MIC(15) out=BLUETOOTH_A2DP(8) rate=48000
```

`dumpsys audio` independently reported `bt_a2dp(80)` as the active output route.

> **`BLUETOOTH_SCO(7)` was present in the device list and was correctly not selected.** This is the
> exact hazard [ADR-0004](../adr/0004-media-path-only.md) exists to prevent, and the output filter
> declined it without any special handling. First real evidence that the media-path-only design
> holds on hardware.

## Quiet-room behaviour (hypothesis H4 / risk R2)

**Pass.** Room tone and small sounds were audible; no cut to digital silence, no obvious AGC
pumping.

This matters more than it looks: the device reports `PROPERTY_SUPPORT_AUDIO_SOURCE_UNPROCESSED =
false`, so **there is no input-source fallback on this phone**. Had ColorOS's `MIC` processing gated
the room, [risk R2](../risks.md) would have landed with nothing to switch to. It did not.

## Drift

From the first session's stop log, ~26.7 s of continuous monitoring:

```text
monitor stopped: framesIn=1280640 framesOut=1280640
```

Frames in and out **exactly equal** — no drop, no pad, no correction needed at this timescale. Says
nothing about hours; that is Scenario G.

**Underruns: not meaningfully measured.** Only one stats sample survived, taken at t=0 before any
audio had flowed. ColorOS is extremely chatty and rotated the later samples out of the logcat ring
buffer. *Lesson for Phase 6: read counters from the app's own UI or a file, not from logcat.*

## Finding — the output buffer was sized wrong

The platform's **minimum** `AudioTrack` buffer on this A2DP route is 20 622 bytes ≈ **215 ms**: the
platform has itself budgeted for the Bluetooth link. [ADR-0006](../adr/0006-audio-format-and-buffering.md)
then doubled it to ~430 ms.

That 2× rule came from built-in-speaker intuition, where minimums are ~20 ms and doubling is
genuinely free. On A2DP it bought no glitch resistance and cost ~215 ms. The tester's "noticeably
delayed but usable" matches the arithmetic rather than contradicting it.

Fixed in `d122d43`, recorded as [ADR-0009](../adr/0009-buffer-sizing-measured.md) superseding
ADR-0006: capture keeps 2×, output takes the platform minimum. **Re-test of latency is pending** —
the corrected build is installed but the earbuds were disconnected before it could be measured.

## Verdict

**Phase 2 gate: PASSED.** The project's central question is answered on hardware:

> Can I put the phone in another room and reliably hear its microphone through my Bluetooth earbud?
> **Yes.**

Four of five gate conditions pass. The fifth — latency within budget — failed, the cause was
identified from the evidence log, fixed, and awaits re-measurement.
